# Devotional editorial review

This reference owns editorial quality only. Use specialist Skills for source disclosure, passage-faithfulness classification, quotation/permission checks, and multi-asset scope.

1. Confirm requested mode and quantity. A plan must remain a plan; a full week must contain five finished days; a single day must not expand into a week. If a prior plan explicitly deferred writing until approval and the user then approved it, confirm that the pending build was executed rather than merely acknowledged.
2. Before producing or approving any named, numbered, dated, or scheduled reading-plan week/day, verify that its readings, titles, Main Goal, Core Teaching, day roles, and cautions came from the exact active Project entry or another accessible controlling source. Prior-chat memory, model memory, completed weeks, and remembered schedules do not satisfy this gate. If the active source is unavailable, stop the reading-plan build rather than reconstructing it from memory.
3. Compare the actual output with the selected reading-plan entries or supplied handoff. Preserve locked readings, titles, goal, teaching, rhythm, and cautions. Flag meaningful source conflicts rather than silently editing the plan.
4. Check whether each day has a distinct role, text-dependent burden, proportionate application, and a natural reflection/prayer/response progression.
5. Assess continuity only against material actually supplied. Keep Inherited Truth, New Contribution, and Forward Movement in an optional editor-facing note, never as unexplained labels inside public prose.
6. Review body length under the active requirement. When tightening, remove repetition before text-rooted substance. Audit reports identify the body count (measured or estimated), range status, and paragraphs best suited for trimming.
7. Run a prose-normalization check. Devotional bodies should be paragraph-dominant and connected, with ordinary exposition, explanation, transition, and application developed in unified paragraphs. As a working norm, most body paragraphs should contain 3-6 sentences when the material supports it. Ordinary one-sentence paragraphs are exceptions and should normally total no more than two per devotional body. Do not allow consecutive one-sentence paragraphs for ordinary exposition, application, or transitions. Scan every six consecutive paragraphs; if three or more are ordinary one-sentence paragraphs, merge related material unless they form a clearly intentional quotation, list, refrain, climax, Scripture block, or response sequence. The final body should read as continuous narrative prose rather than stacked speaking notes. Also check repetitive hooks, imagery, emotional movement, stock contrast patterns, cliches, unnecessary em dashes, and repeated prayer structures.
8. Confirm each Finish This Sentence stem is unique, incomplete, and grows from that day's developed application. Avoid identical openings across consecutive days.
9. Ensure alternate titles remain alternatives, social summaries include the title and introduce no new ideas, and the final response contains no unrequested downstream package.

When an audit is requested, return concise findings by severity, what should be preserved, and concrete revision recommendations. When revision is requested, deliver the revised devotional first and only necessary notes. Do not claim cross-series comparison, source verification, or theological review beyond the work actually performed.
